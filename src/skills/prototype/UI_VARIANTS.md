# UI Variant Prototypes

Use this branch when the question is open-ended and interaction or information structure cannot be settled from prose alone.

Create two or three interactive variants representing every credible structural hypothesis needed to discriminate the question. Add a third only when a genuinely distinct third hypothesis remains plausible. Each variant must express a different structural hypothesis relevant to the question, such as navigation model, information hierarchy, task flow, disclosure strategy, or interaction pattern. Color, typography, spacing, and decoration alone do not make variants structurally distinct.

Keep shared content and evaluation conditions comparable. Make switching among variants quick and preserve enough instrumentation or observation prompts to compare them against the same question.

Do not manufacture a winner. Report what evaluators observed, which criteria favored each variant, where evidence conflicted, and what remained untested. Do not create a cosmetic or straw variant to reach a count. When only two hypotheses are credible, name the excluded structural possibilities and why they would not change the decision.
