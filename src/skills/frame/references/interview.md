# Interview

Resolve one bounded human-knowledge objective. Research facts first, then ask only for knowledge, authority, preference, or judgment the human must supply.

## Enter the interview

Enter this operation only when Frame terrain leaves boundary-changing questions that require human knowledge, judgment, preference, or authority. If supplied evidence and authority already converge, record the convergence and continue toward Frame sufficiency without an interview. When an interview is required, tell the user that evidence gathering is complete enough to begin and that the Frame remains active. State the interview objective and use the decision-card sequence estimate for orientation. Begin with the earliest answer that changes the meaning or scope of later questions.

Do not replace the interview with a synopsis followed by a passive list of questions. A synopsis may orient the first decision card, but the interaction continues one answer at a time unless the user pauses, redirects, or explicitly opts out of guided questioning.

Probe selectively through these gap lenses:

- Meaning
- Evidence
- Authority
- Consistency
- Consequence
- Counterfactual
- Durability
- Attachment
- Scope

Ask in dependency order. Apply `decision-card` to every context-bearing human question; it owns the delta-first format, proportional depth, visible confirmation scope, and answer interpretation rules.

Keep deeper modeling and rationale in Frame/Case state. When options cannot be distinguished, recommend the next discriminating evidence, question, deferral, or reframing neutrally.

Treat the bounded interview as one operation. Keep accepted answers in conversational working state and ask the next question without writing Frame or Case files after every answer. The natural persistence boundary is when the interview objective is resolved, blocked, deliberately deferred, preserved as contested, the human pauses or redirects, compaction/handoff risk requires a checkpoint, or the conversation leaves the interview for research, prototyping, deliberation, or another operation. Before switching operations or following the new direction, reconcile the accepted batch and dispositions so an abandoned thread does not lose its decisions. Persist an individual answer immediately only when the human requests it or a consequential external effect requires a durable prerequisite.

Continue until that boundary. Put adjacent concerns in Frame Discovery rather than expanding the interview.

The interview is not complete while an unanswered question can still change the outcome, external guarantee, exclusion, accepted risk, authority boundary, or compatibility commitment. At completion, restate the accepted boundary briefly, name any deliberate deferrals or contested points, and persist the batch before claiming Frame sufficiency.
